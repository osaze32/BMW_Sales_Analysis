# Project Overview

---

This project examines automobile sales data to understand overall performance and market trends. It looks at sales volumes, revenue, and growth over time, as well as how different models and regions contribute to total sales. The purpose is to give a clear picture of how the market is performing and where the strongest opportunities lie.

Beyond performance numbers, the analysis also considers customer preferences, such as which fuel types, transmissions, or colors are most popular, and how price and engine size affect demand. By combining sales results with these insights, the project aims to show not just what happened in the market, but why, helping guide smarter product, pricing, and regional strategies.

# Data Structure

---

The dataset contains automobile sales records with details on model, year, region, color, fuel type, transmission, engine size, mileage, price, sales volume, and sales classification. It provides a broad view of sales performance and customer preferences across different markets.

**Key Details:**

- Link to dataset: https://www.kaggle.com/datasets/eshummalik/bmw-sales-dataset/data
- 50,000 rows and 11 columns
- Time span: 2010 to 2024
- Key fields: Model, Year, Region, Color
- Inconsistent product naming formats identified
- No duplicate records detected
- Data cleaning included imputation and selective removal of missing entries

# Executive Summary

BMW’s sales analysis shows **253 million units sold** across 50,000 orders, generating about **$19 trillion in revenue**. The **7 Series, i8, and X1** led model sales, while **Asia was the top region**, followed closely by Europe and North America. Despite South America recording the lowest sales, performance was balanced across regions.

Over time, sales peaked in **2022 before dropping in 2023**, reflecting cyclical shifts. Consumer demand favored **red cars, hybrids, high-class sales, and large engines (3L–5L)**, which dominated results. These patterns highlight BMW’s strong market presence and alignment with trends in premium and sustainable vehicles.

# Key Insights

---

The analysis begins with overall sales metrics, then narrows into model performance, regional variations, and yearly trends, before drilling down to key product attributes such as color, fuel type, sales class, and engine size. This progression reveals both the scale of demand and the factors shaping it.

- Across all years in the dataset, a total of **253 million vehicles** were sold through 50,000 orders. With an average selling price of **$75,034.60**, this translated into approximately **$19 trillion in sales revenue**, underscoring the scale of demand across the automotive market.
- When comparing models, the **7 Series** led sales with **23.8 million units**, narrowly ahead of the **i8 (23.4 million)** and the **X1 (23.4 million)**. The small gap of just **1.4 million units** between the most and least demanded models shows that demand was broadly balanced across product lines, rather than dominated by a single flagship.
- From a regional perspective, **Asia ranked first with 43.0 million units sold**, followed closely by **Europe (42.6 million)** and **North America (42.4 million)**. Even the lowest-performing region, **South America (41.6 million)**, was not far behind. The tight spread of only **1.4 million units** between the highest and lowest regions suggests a globally consistent demand profile.
- Looking over time, sales showed steady trends with noticeable peaks. The strongest year was **2022**, recording **17.9 million units**, before a sharp decline to **16.3 million in 2023**—a drop of **1.65 million units year-over-year**. This highlights the presence of market cycles and potential external shocks influencing yearly performance.
- In terms of product attributes, **red cars generated the highest sales revenue** (42.8 million), with **silver and white** close behind. **Black vehicles** had the lowest revenue (41.7 million), creating only a **1 million unit gap** between the top and bottom colors, suggesting color preference was not a major driver of sales.
- Shifts in **fuel type** were more revealing: **Hybrid vehicles led with 64.5 million units**, slightly ahead of **petrol (63.3 million)** and **electric (63.2 million)**, while **diesel lagged at 62.4 million**. This reflects the growing market transition toward **sustainable fuel options**, with hybrids and EVs gradually overtaking traditional engines.
- Sales classification showed that the **high-sales class dominated with 129.6 million units**, representing **over half (51.1%) of total sales**, compared to **medium (33%)** and **low (16%)**. This indicates that premium-performing products continue to anchor the bulk of sales.
- Finally, by engine size, vehicles with **large engines (3L–5L)** outperformed all others, contributing **148.2 million units** and making up nearly **60% of total sales**. This trend emphasizes customer preference for higher-powered vehicles, despite the industry’s shift toward smaller and more efficient alternatives.

# Recommendations

---

- **Expand Hybrid and EV Offerings** – With hybrids leading sales and EVs close behind, BMW should increase investment in electrified models to capture growing demand for sustainable vehicles.
- **Leverage Regional Strengths** – Asia, Europe, and North America drive the majority of sales; targeted marketing and tailored model launches in these regions could maximize returns while strategies to boost sales in South America can reduce performance gaps.
- **Capitalize on Premium Demand** – High-class sales and large-engine models dominate results. BMW should reinforce its premium positioning with luxury-focused features while also offering performance-oriented hybrids to align prestige with sustainability.
